## The details

The card called **Road to Riches** on the Routing tab's Plan page, and the
`plot_exploration_route` tool behind it.

### What you fill in

| Box | What it means | Out of the box |
|---|---|---|
| **Stops** | How many systems the tour visits | 10 |
| **Radius (ly)** | How far from the start it may look | 500 |
| **Least worth stopping for (cr)** | The floor a body must clear to be included | 500,000 |
| **Come back to the start** | Whether the last stop is where the first was | Ticked |

It starts from where you are. There is no destination box, because there is no destination — the
route is the point.

### The three dials pull against each other

Raising **Least worth stopping for** does not make the route more valuable on its own; it makes the
pool of candidate bodies smaller. If the pool goes below the number of **stops** you asked for, the
only way to get them back is a wider **radius** — which means longer legs between them.

The shape that usually disappoints is a high floor and a small radius: a short list of enormous
payouts scattered so far apart that the flying costs more than the scanning earns.

### Take a Detailed Surface Scanner

The figures a route quotes assume you will map what you arrive at. Honking and flying on earns a
fraction of it. Directive 47 does not check whether one is fitted, so this is the one piece of
preparation the plan cannot do for you.

### What it is worth is an estimate, and estimates go stale

Body values come from what the service knows, and a system somebody scanned last week is a system
whose first-discovery bonus is gone. A route is a good list of places to look, not a promise about
your balance.

### It is a job, and it can be refused

Like the Neutron Plotter, this is submitted and queued rather than asked and answered, with a
ninety-second budget. A Road to Riches loop came back on the first poll when measured on
2026-08-14. Past the budget the answer says so, and says what to change: fewer stops, or a smaller
radius.

The tool schema and the service it calls are on
[Route planning](capabilities/routes.html) — one page for all three planners, because that part
really is shared.
